# Mission Reflection

Throughout Mission 3: Become a Multi‑Cloud Explorer, I gained valuable insights into cloud computing and the importance of understanding multiple platforms. At the beginning, I explored [AWS](ca://s?q=AWS_strengths), [Azure](ca://s?q=Azure_strengths), and [GCP](ca://s?q=GCP_strengths) individually, documenting their core services and unique strengths. This helped me see how each provider has its own advantages depending on business needs.

Checkpoint 3 allowed me to compare the platforms side by side, which highlighted differences in pricing, scalability, and integration. In Checkpoint 4, I applied this knowledge to real‑world scenarios by recommending platforms for specific clients. This step taught me how to align technical features with business requirements. Checkpoint 5 further deepened my understanding by matching equivalent services across providers, showing how similar solutions can be achieved in different ecosystems.

Checkpoint 6 was especially useful because I created a decision matrix. This exercise required me to think critically about which platform best fits certain contexts, such as startups, enterprises, or AI‑focused projects. It reinforced the idea that there is no single “best” cloud provider — the right choice depends on the situation.

Checkpoint 7 gave me hands‑on experience with Linux commands in a cloud environment. Running commands like `uname -a`, `lscpu`, `free -h`, and `df -h` helped me understand how to gather system information and connect it to cloud hosting options. This practical step tied the theory back to real technical skills.

Overall, this mission improved both my technical knowledge and my ability to make strategic recommendations. I learned that cloud computing is not just about technology, but also about aligning solutions with business goals. This reflection shows how each checkpoint built on the previous one, leading to a comprehensive understanding of multi‑cloud strategies.
